# Agent instructions — academic website

## Working agreement

- Follow the user’s latest instructions. Keep durable design and workflow decisions here; do not duplicate biographies, publication records, metrics, or personal favorites.
- Use the PDF and LaTeX CV in `files/cv/` for factual records, together with approved sources in `content/` and explicit user corrections. Do not invent dates, interests, claims, or publication status. Preserve author order and distinguish accepted work from preprints. Never call an arXiv-only paper “accepted”, in news, badges, or copy: it is a preprint. `is_preprint()` in the builder suppresses the Accepted badge and fails the build if a news entry calls a preprint accepted.
- Maintain this guide in place. Do not create additional Markdown guides unless requested.
- Keep work inside this project. Verify the Git root before staging; never stage the parent home directory. Do not push or publish unless requested.
- `publication_input_iut_website.csv` is the import file for the IUT faculty publications profile. Keep its `Type,Date,Info` header, use ISO dates, preserve approved author order, and format `Info` as a complete readable citation with the paper title, venue or repository, year, and a final canonical URL to the external publication record. Classify every arXiv-only preprint as `Others`; an arXiv-hosted copy of a work with an approved workshop or conference venue keeps that venue classification. Never use the personal website or IUT profile URL as the citation link. Validate CSV quoting and confirm that every `Info` containing arXiv has `Type` set to `Others` before importing it.

## Design philosophy

Create an original, reading-focused academic website. Help visitors identify the person, understand their work, find outputs, and make contact. Give Home, Publications, CV, and Personal distinct purposes within consistent navigation.

- Use a clean, Apple-like surface: near-white page (`--bg #f5f5f7`), white cards (`--card #ffffff`), ink `#1d1d1f`, muted `#6e6e73`, soft `--rule`/`--fill` greys, and a single blue accent (`--accent #0068d1`, `--accent-strong #0071e3`). Dark mode is `#000` with `#1d1d1f` cards. Do not introduce a rainbow of button colours or warm/cream palettes.
- Body and headings use the system sans stack (`-apple-system`, `BlinkMacSystemFont`, `SF Pro Display`/`SF Pro Text`, `Helvetica Neue`, `Segoe UI`, `system-ui`), with the same stack on meta, eyebrows, dates, tags, nav, and buttons. No external font service is loaded. The bundled Literata files are **not** used by the web pages: keep `literata.ttf` and `literata-light.ttf` only because `files/cv/tariq.tex` compiles the CV from them — never delete or re-encode those two — and `assets/fonts/literata.woff2` is retained but unreferenced.
- Establish hierarchy through typography and spacing. Keep comfortable line lengths and make supporting metadata quieter than titles. Avoid generic slogans, decorative straplines, and repeated information.
- Keep substantive content and navigation usable without JavaScript. Use JavaScript only to enhance theme controls, touch press feedback, publication drawers, figure carousels, and active-section highlighting.
- Make layouts responsive, preserve semantic reading order, and support keyboard focus, readable contrast, and reduced motion.
- Keep documentation and site content self-contained, without references to an external design-inspiration website.

## Page and interaction rules

- **Home:** preserve the approved biography, research-interest cards, and personal section. Keep a single “Recent” section after the research-interest cards and before Personal: every `news.json` entry through `{{NEWS}}`, sorted newest first by its `Mon YYYY` date in the builder (file order does not matter), with the shared `.section-heading` pattern and an “All publications →” link. No expander, no cap, and no selected-publications block. Keep desktop biography text at 19px. The name `h1` is the largest element on Home (about 3.5rem, weight 700) with a short accent rule beneath it, so it leads over the action buttons. On mobile, use name heading → portrait/caption → biography in DOM order. Center the portrait at up to 200px wide with natural proportions, hide the repeated small name, and retain role, institution, and location below the photo.
- **Research interests:** there is no Research page; research is presented on Home and Publications. One shared set of four categories, in this order — Misinformation & harmful content, LLM evaluation & bias, Low-resource & Bangla NLP, Accessibility & sign language. These are the Home card titles; renaming or reordering them means also updating the Home biography's “My work spans…” sentence, the Home meta description, the JSON-LD `knowsAbout` list, and the `llms.txt` summary in the builder, in the same order and wording. The cards link to `publications.html#misinformation`, `#evaluation`, `#bangla`, and `#accessibility`. The builder emits these four anchors before the first paper carrying each area, because entries are grouped by type rather than by area.
- **Home interest cards:** tags carry published venues only — `ACL 2026`, `BLP @ EMNLP 2023`, `CV4A11y @ ICCV 2025`. No arXiv or preprint tags, no dataset, model, or metric keywords.
- **No paper pages:** each paper lives only in its Publications entry, and titles link to the paper itself. Home’s Recent items link to `publications.html#<id>`. The old `publications/<id>/` URLs are generated as noindex redirect stubs to that anchor so existing links still land; they are not in the sitemap, and there is no “Project page” button.
- **Publications:** place large publication/citation/h-index/i10-index metrics directly below the title and lead, without a box, border, or surface fill. Use Google Scholar and Download CV actions. Group entries as Conference papers, Journal articles, Workshop papers, and Preprints, with C/J/W/P numbering and area tags. Preserve paper anchors and Code & data. Calculate publication totals from metadata; show unavailable metrics as dashes, treating zero as a valid value.
- **Publication entries:** marker → status → title → authors → venue → award → tags → one button row in this order: Overview, Paper, Copy BibTeX, Video, Dataset, Code. Every non-preprint shows its venue as a full-width “Accepted” band (gold seal-check icon, uppercase label, venue name on a pale gold ground with a gold left rule) so every entry’s badge is the same width; on phones it stacks to two rows — the seal and label on top, the venue name below — so a long venue never crowds the label. A preprint keeps the plain italic venue line and never gets the badge, which `is_preprint()` enforces. Every accepted badge links its venue name (with the external-link mark) to the official announcement or accepted-papers page via `venue_url` in `content/publications.json` — set this for every new accepted paper at entry time (conference program/findings page, workshop accepted-papers page), and never point it at the paper itself, arXiv, or this site. There is a single Paper button — the publisher record, else arXiv, else the PDF — and no separate arXiv or PDF buttons, since the paper page reaches both. “Overview” is a toggle and “Copy BibTeX” a split button whose chevron expands the BibTeX. Both open drawers below the row: the overview holds the “In short” callout (`page.tldr`) with the full abstract collapsed, then the paper’s figures with captions — every figure from `page` (teaser, section figures, finding figures) in order, deduplicated. Two or more figures form a sideways scroll-snap carousel (arrows, dots, arrow keys, “n / N” on phones; off-screen slides are inert); one figure shows alone. Each figure keeps its “View full size” caption link. Without JavaScript the drawers are native `<details>` with their own summaries and the carousel still swipes, and the JS-only toggles stay `hidden`. Captions must stand alone: no “below”/“above” references. Everything shown comes from the paper; do not add claims or numbers it does not state. Figures are cropped from the paper PDFs into `files/papers/<id>/` as WebP.
- **CV:** retain approved appointments without restoring removed entries. Education records consistently show dates, degree, institution/location, then GPA and rank on a separate results line when available; `Present` already conveys that the M.Sc. is in progress. Teaching Experience contains the Junior Lecturer appointment and one concise job summary; do not list courses or supervision details on the page or PDF. Keep historical course records in `teaching.json` as source data, but do not render them in the CV. Semesters are not shown.
- **CV ordering (PDF and page):** for PhD applications, the record runs Research Interests → Education → Research Publications → Research Experience → Teaching Experience → Industry Experience → Awards. The page mirrors that order with separate Research experience, Teaching experience, and Industry experience chapters. Keep `#experience`, `#appointments`, and `#teaching` as legacy anchors so older links still land; the Junior Lecturer appointment sits inside Teaching experience.
- **CV restraint (graduate applications):** keep Teaching Experience to one sentence summarizing undergraduate teaching and student project supervision; do not mention thesis supervision there or add course lists, supervision details, Skills, Service, or Talks sections, and do not print citation counts or h-index. No separate Datasets & Software section either — it repeats the papers, so each artifact rides on its publication entry as [Code] or [Data]. The RedDot entry reads “Machine Learning Intern” with no task detail, under Industry Experience on the PDF and page alike. Education contains degrees and results only: do not put thesis or supervisor details there. Keep scholarships and awards in Awards & Achievements without repeating them in Education. Link the HSC Talent Pool Scholarship to the Chattogram Board scholarship PDF and describe it as awarded to the top 1.7% among students who received GPA 5.00/5.00 from that board. The approved records include SSC and “Best Student Award (2018),” awarded by the Old Placidians Association for overall academic and co-curricular performance. Label the preprint P to match the site, and claim no submission venue for it. The PDF carries no "Last updated" dateline; it reads as unprofessional on application material.
- **CV actions:** Home, Publications, and CV buttons read “Download CV” (omit “Full” and “PDF”) with the PDF icon and the external-link mark, `data-kind="cv"`, and open `files/cv/tariq.pdf` in a new tab (`target="_blank" rel="noopener"`) — no `download` attribute.
- **Buttons and icons:** one solid accent (blue) button leads each action row — the first `.btn` in a `.link-row` and in `.publication-actions`, and the Paper button in a paper’s link row; every other `.btn` uses the quiet grey `--fill` ground with ink text. Keep each fill at 4.5:1 or better against its text. “See more” routes (section-heading links, Read more) stay plain text links with a sliding arrow. Icons are inline SVG from `ICONS` in `scripts/build.py`: build code calls `icon()`/`resource_button()`, and content files write `{{icon:name}}`, which `render()` expands. External links carry a small trailing ↗ icon, internal routes a trailing →. Do not go back to text glyphs or CSS `::before` markers.
- **Section headings:** section `h2`s (Home `.section-heading`, CV chapters, Publications groups and Code & data, Personal panels) are plain headings; the Home `.section-heading` sits above a hairline rule with its “See more” link on the right. The “On this page” navigation (not the headings) is what stays pinned under the sticky header.
- **Section navigation:** Publications, CV, and Personal share “On this page” navigation. Keep every link visible below the sticky header and highlight the active section while scrolling. On phones every such menu is one swipeable sticky row (no eyebrow, 44px targets, edge shadows on the side with more links) that scrolls itself to keep the active link in view — never a multi-row grid, which eats the screen. Preserve stable anchors and correct scroll offsets.
- **Personal:** order topics Anime → Movies → Books → Sports, matching homepage links. Preserve the approved introduction and owner-supplied rankings. Describe collections as all-time favorites, not complete viewing histories; derive counts from supplied entries rather than inventing picks.
- **Favorites grid:** five columns on desktop (over 1000px), three on tablets (601–1000px), two on phones (600px and below).
- **Favorite cards:** each whole card, including image, title, author, and padding, is one Wikipedia link. No repeated provider labels or nested links. On hover (pointer devices only, via `@media(hover:hover)`), lift the whole card up, scale the artwork slightly, and deepen its soft drop shadow, without borders or background changes. Preserve visible keyboard focus and disable the motion for reduced-motion preferences.
- **Artwork:** load covers remotely; do not store artwork binaries in the repository. Keep provenance in metadata and the shared provider/rights-holder note. Remote hosting does not imply a copyright license.

## Source map

- `content/home.html`, `cv.html`, `personal.html`: approved page content and templates. The Home biography ends at the research-focus sentence, with no lab membership line (that record lives on the CV page).
- `content/publications.json`: paper metadata, author order, types, status, topics, and resources, plus each paper's `page` content. The site renders only `page.tldr`, `page.links`, and the figures (teaser, section and finding figures); the remaining section, finding, table, and video text is kept as source data. `award_url` links a named award to its official announcement on both the Publications list and paper page. `page.links` adds extra buttons to the entry.
- `content/scholar-metrics.json`: supplied/verified metrics and recorded date; `null` means unavailable.
- `content/personal.json`: ordered favorites; `image` is remote artwork, `source`/`provider` record provenance, and `url` is the Wikipedia card destination.
- `content/teaching.json`: historical teaching records by term, not rendered in the CV. `content/news.json`: historical updates, not shown on Home.
- `publication_input_iut_website.csv`: import-ready IUT publication records; this is maintained separately from `content/publications.json` because the IUT profile has its own type/date/citation fields. Follow the CSV rules in `readme.md` when adding rows.
- `scripts/build.py`: shared HTML through `render()`, page metadata, publication groups/metrics, and favorite-card rendering. It also emits `404.html` (noindex), `sitemap.xml`, and `robots.txt`, and injects JSON-LD through `schemas` — the `Person` node on Home, and on Publications a `@graph` of that same node plus one `ScholarlyArticle` per paper (its `@id` is `publications.html#<id>`), with his authorship pointing at the Person's `@id` — plus Twitter card metadata on every page. These generated files are build output; edit the builder, not them.
- `assets/style.css`: design tokens, base typography, and layout. `assets/interactions.css`: buttons, drawers, carousel, section tones, responsive overrides, reduced motion, and print.
- `assets/app.js`: theme toggle (with reduced-motion-safe touch press feedback), publication drawers, figure carousels, and `.page-contents` scroll highlighting through `aria-current="location"`.
- `profile.jpg`: portrait, 699x715 and about 150 KB. It is the homepage's largest-paint element, so keep it near this size; On desktop and tablet it shows uncropped at its natural 699:715 ratio in a 300px column (220px at 601–800px) beside the text, spanning the name and biography rows; this resolution covers 2x of that. Do not return to a tall cropped frame. `files/cv/`: factual CV sources and downloadable PDF.
- `readme.md`: website documentation. `github-profile/readme.md`: separate prepared profile README for the `mdtariquzzaman/mdtariquzzaman` repository; keep consistent with approved content and publish only when requested. Its shields.io badges use green `425b46` for identity and venues, gold `a3864e` for interests and the award, and grey `68665d` for paper resources, all `style=flat-square`. Verify any new badge actually embeds its `logo=` slug, since shields silently drops unknown ones (LinkedIn's mark is unavailable, so that badge carries no logo). Keep blank lines between a paper's title, description, and badge row: a GitHub `.md` file collapses single newlines, which is what makes each badge row render as one row.
