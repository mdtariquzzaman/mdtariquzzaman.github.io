## Design philosophy

Create a fun, nerdy academic portfolio with a coherent GBA-era RPG menu style. Home, Publications, the CV webpage, and Personal share the same visual world while helping visitors identify the researcher, understand his work, find outputs, and make contact.

- Keep authentic GBA menu construction with quiet pixel outlines, stepped corners, and shallow inset edges. Use a parchment-and-teal day palette: parchment backdrop `#f2e9d8`, paper panels `#fff7e8`, ink `#1e2a32`, deep-teal frames `#29434a`, sage title strips `#bdd3c7`, a dark-teal header strip `#203d43`, and terracotta accent `#a64a35`. Night uses indigo backdrop `#181d30`, panels `#262c43`, light paper text, and coral accent `#e7b3a4`. Keep `theme-color` metas and `app.js` in step with both `--bg` values. Carry the same frame language through all pages, navigation, drawers, and footer. Use the paired minimal pixel-sky SVG backdrops over full-width CSS bands; `scenes.js` replaces them with a live canvas whose first frame matches them. Do not restore a green world, checker grid, arcade neon, CRT effects, or large floating shadows.
- Use self-hosted Pixel Operator Mono for mixed-case pixel headings, menu labels, and controls, and IBM Plex Mono for readable body copy and long metadata. No external font service is loaded. Preserve upstream font licenses. The bundled Literata files are **not** used by the web pages: keep `literata.ttf` and `literata-light.ttf` because `files/cv/tariq.tex` compiles the CV from them — never delete or re-encode those two — and retain the unreferenced `assets/fonts/literata.woff2`.
- Establish compact hierarchy through framed title strips, aligned record fields, typography, and spacing. Keep body copy and long titles readable, supporting metadata quieter, and paragraphs at comfortable line lengths. Copy may be shortened to remove repetition while preserving approved facts. Do not invent RPG levels, health bars, quests, achievements, or other personal claims.
- Keep the real portrait in the Home profile window and use a separate scene/social window beside it. Its original pixel still-life contains only a bookshelf with books, a window, a coffee cup on the sill, and plain wall/sky; no human, desk, laptop, language notes, or other props. Generate matching day/night versions with crisp GBA pixels and the same composition. Code-drawn overlays may animate what the scene already contains: steam over the cup, sky and stars beyond the window glass, and light in the sunbeams. They must not add props. The quiet Japanese influence is limited to wood, paper, and restraint, without garden scenery, spiritual symbols, or personal philosophy claims. Do not restore an “In the lab” card, CSS room, map hotspots, or “Tariq’s lab” branding. Visitors use familiar labels and direct navigation; do not gate content behind movement, a start screen, sound, or typing animation.
- Keep substantive content and navigation usable without JavaScript. Use JavaScript only to enhance theme controls, touch press feedback, publication drawers, figure carousels, active-section highlighting, the reading gauge, and decorative pixel animation.
- Keep animation subtle, decorative, and classic pixel art: whole-pixel movement, low frame rates, ordered dithering instead of alpha blends, no smoothing, glow, or easing. Scenes pause off-screen and in hidden tabs. Under reduced motion, or while the header's remembered pause button holds motion (`window.siteMotion`, `data-motion`), show still frames and skip CSS animation and the day/night dither dissolve. Never carry content, gate navigation, or add sound through animation. Rare sky and window events stay occasional and brief.
- Accepted venue badges use a continuous seven-second smooth tonal loop in their background and fixed inset rim, as requested by the owner. This is an exception to stepped decorative motion: the seal, text, and frame stay stationary, with no sparkle or bounce. Pause the loop off-screen, in hidden tabs, under reduced motion, and while the header motion control is held.
- Make layouts responsive, preserve semantic reading order, and support keyboard focus, readable contrast, and reduced motion.
- Keep documentation and site content self-contained. Record asset sources and licenses in `readme.md`; do not depend on an external design-inspiration website.

## Source map

- `content/home.html`, `cv.html`, `personal.html`: approved page content and templates. The Home biography closes with the Systems and Software Lab membership line, linked to the lab site; the CV keeps the full appointment record.
- `content/publications.json`: paper metadata, author order, types, status, topics, and resources, plus each paper's `page` content. The site renders only `page.tldr`, `page.links`, and the figures (teaser, section and finding figures); the remaining section, finding, table, and video text is kept as source data. `award_url` links a named award to its official announcement on both the Publications list and paper page. `page.links` adds extra buttons to the entry.
- `content/scholar-metrics.json`: supplied/verified metrics and recorded date; `null` means unavailable.
- `content/personal.json`: ordered favorites with optional authors; `url` is the Wikipedia list-row destination. Keep any supplied artwork provenance in the data, but do not render copyrighted or unapproved artwork in the Personal lists.
- `content/teaching.json`: historical teaching records by term, not rendered in the CV. `content/news.json`: updates rendered on Home, with the newest three visible and older entries in “Earlier updates”.
- `publication_input_iut_website.csv`: import-ready IUT publication records; this is maintained separately from `content/publications.json` because the IUT profile has its own type/date/citation fields. Follow the CSV rules in `readme.md` when adding rows.
- `scripts/build.py`: shared HTML through `render()`, page metadata, publication groups/metrics, and favorite-list rendering. It also emits `404.html` (noindex), `sitemap.xml`, and `robots.txt`, and injects JSON-LD through `schemas` — the `Person` node on Home, and on Publications a `@graph` of that same node plus one `ScholarlyArticle` per paper (its `@id` is `publications.html#<id>`), with his authorship pointing at the Person's `@id` — plus Twitter card metadata on every page. These generated files are build output; edit the builder, not them.
- `assets/style.css`: design tokens, base typography, header controls, and layout. `assets/interactions.css`: buttons, drawers, carousel, small stepped animations (menu-cursor nudge, icon hop, window-open drawers, accepted-seal glint), the day/night dither dissolve, section tones, responsive overrides, reduced motion, and print.
- `assets/app.js`: `window.siteMotion` and the header motion toggle, theme toggle with the dither dissolve and reduced-motion-safe touch press feedback, reading gauge, accepted-seal glint, publication drawers, figure carousels, and `.page-contents` scroll highlighting through `aria-current="location"`. It loads before `scenes.js`.
- `assets/scenes.js`: one requestAnimationFrame loop for the canvas pixel scenes. It draws the live sky on every page (drifting clouds and occasional birds by day; twinkling stars and occasional shooting stars by night) and the Home still-life overlays on `[data-scene="reading-corner"]`. Sky geometry must match `sky-day.svg` and `sky-night.svg`; overlay masks are read from the GIF pixels.
